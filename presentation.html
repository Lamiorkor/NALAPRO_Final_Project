Classifying 20 Newsgroups: Applying Word2Vec, TF-IDF, BERT and Llama-3

A compact tour through the experiments, what worked, what disappointed me, and what I learned about prompt-based classification.

Naa Lamiorkor Boye NALAPRO Final Project
Initial Problem

Can different NLP representations reliably classify discussion posts into 20 topics?

The challenge was to map noisy user-written posts into fine-grained newsgroup categories.

Why it is interesting

Some topics are obvious, but many are close together: religion, politics, computer hardware, operating systems, and technical science discussions.

What I compared

Classic vectorization, supervised BERT fine-tuning, BERT domain adaptation, Llama-3 prompting, and a bonus QLoRA fine-tuning run.

Dataset

20 Newsgroups, 20 classes, thousands of short documents

20newsgroup categories
9,912non-empty training documents after split
7,317cleaned test documents for BERT comparison
2,000stratified Llama prompt-evaluation subset

Main preprocessing

  • Removed headers, footers, and quoted text for the main experiments.
  • Removed empty documents after cleaning.
  • Used weighted F1 because this is a multi-class problem.

Extra Llama analysis

I also ran a full-context zero-shot Llama experiment with headers and quotes included. This was not a fair main comparison, but it helped explain Llama's behavior.

Experimental Roadmap

I moved from simple representations to task-specific transformer training

1

Word2Vec

Train embeddings, average word vectors, classify with a simple NN.

2

TF-IDF

Use sparse term-weight features with the same NN architecture.

3

BERT

Fine-tune BERT-base directly for 20-class classification.

4

MLM + BERT

Adapt BERT with masked language modeling, then fine-tune.

5

Llama-3

Try zero-shot, few-shot, full-context analysis, and QLoRA bonus.

Llama-3 Prompting

Prompting Llama-3 was useful, but weaker than supervised learning

34.20%

31.75%
Zero-shot, full context

Why the scores were low

  • Llama was used as a generative classifier, not fine-tuned.
  • Some labels overlap semantically.
  • Cleaned documents removed metadata that Llama could exploit.
  • Few-shot examples can add noise when there are 20 classes.

Important lesson: prompt consistency mattered. Few-shot improved only after examples and requested output format both used label numbers.

What Went Well, What Did Not

The strongest results came from task-specific training

Worked well

  • TF-IDF n-grams improved the simple NN.
  • BERT fine-tuning was robust.
  • MLM adaptation gave a small gain.

Was harder

  • Llama zero-shot was sensitive to labels and prompts.
  • Few-shot examples did not automatically help.
  • Prompt outputs needed careful parsing.

Surprised me

  • Full context helped Llama slightly.
  • Fine-tuning Llama changed performance dramatically.
  • Simple baselines remained competitive.
What I Learned

Good results depended on matching the method to the task

Main takeaways

  • Representations shape what the classifier can learn.
  • Supervised fine-tuning is much stronger than prompting for this dataset.
  • LLMs can classify, but prompt design and evaluation setup matter a lot.
  • Domain adaptation can help, but gains may be modest.

Interesing Quote

"How you represent text strongly affects what the classifier can learn."
Future Work

If I extended this project, I would focus on fairer and deeper Llama evaluation

1. Evaluate QLoRA on a larger stratified test set

The bonus result was promising, but the test set was smaller than the BERT evaluation.

2. Try better class descriptions for Llama prompts

The labels alone may not define the subtle boundaries between related newsgroups.

3. Analyze errors by class family

The most interesting mistakes happen between related topics such as politics, religion, and computer hardware.

Conclusion

The best full-test model was MLM-adapted BERT, while QLoRA showed why fine-tuning matters for LLMs

74.01%best full-test accuracy
0.7370best full-test weighted F1
34.20%Llama zero-shot cleaned-text accuracy
75.00%bonus QLoRA subset accuracy
My biggest takeaway: Llama-3 is powerful, but for fine-grained classification, task adaptation and evaluation design matter more than model size alone.
